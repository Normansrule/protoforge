# Mechanisms Lab (`#/mechanisms`, engine module `mechanisms`)

Design things that move — and check that they keep moving. The Mechanisms
Lab solves four-bar and slider-crank linkages by vector loop closure
(Freudenstein's equation), sizes helical compression, extension and torsion
springs the way Shigley does, generates true involute spur gears by rolling a
rounded ISO 53 rack (profile shift, trochoidal root fillet, undercut), meshes
them along the line of action, designs cam motion programs with displacement,
velocity, acceleration and jerk (SVAJ) diagrams, and designs the **Iris spring
hinge** (component HW1): torque against opening angle, clamp on the head,
spring stress, Goodman fatigue safety factor and life for a stated usage.
Everything is animated (play, pause, scrub; paused under
`prefers-reduced-motion`) and every result can be downloaded.

Snap fits, screws, bosses and living hinges are already in the
[Structures Lab](structures.md) (Calculators tab) and are not duplicated.

## The maths

**Four-bar.** With crank a, coupler b, rocker c and ground d, the loop
`a e^{iθ₂} + b e^{iθ₃} − c e^{iθ₄} − d = 0` gives Freudenstein's equation
`K₁ cos θ₄ − K₂ cos θ₂ + K₃ = cos(θ₂ − θ₄)`, `K₁ = d/a`, `K₂ = d/c`,
`K₃ = (a² − b² + c² + d²)/(2ac)`, solved with the tangent half-angle
substitution `θ₄ = 2 atan2(−B ∓ √(B² − 4AC), 2A)` (Norton eqs. 4.10–4.13; the
coupler angle likewise with K₄, K₅). Velocities
`ω₃ = a ω₂ sin(θ₄ − θ₂)/(b sin(θ₃ − θ₄))`, `ω₄ = a ω₂ sin(θ₂ − θ₃)/(c sin(θ₄ − θ₃))`
and accelerations (Norton eq. 7.12) differentiate the loop. A negative
discriminant means the loop cannot close — beyond a toggle. Grashof:
`S + L ≤ P + Q`. Transmission angle μ is the acute angle between coupler and
rocker. Toggle crank angles: `cos θ₂ = (a² + d² − (b ± c)²)/(2ad)`.

**Slider-crank.** `x = r cos θ + √(l² − (r sin θ − e)²)`, differentiated
exactly (`a_max ≈ r ω²(1 + r/l)` only for comparison).

**Three-position synthesis** (Norton §5.8). For each dyad
`W(e^{iβ₂} − 1) + Z(e^{iα₂} − 1) = δ₂`, `W(e^{iβ₃} − 1) + Z(e^{iα₃} − 1) = δ₃`
— a 2 × 2 complex linear system — then `O₂ = P₁ − Z − W`, `O₄ = P₁ − S − U`.

**Springs** (Shigley ch. 10). `S_ut = A/d^m` (Table 10-4 SI constants);
`S_sy ≈ 0.45 S_ut` for music and hard-drawn wire, `0.35 S_ut` for austenitic
stainless (Table 10-6); `k = d⁴G/(8D³N_a)`; `τ = K_B 8FD/(πd³)` with
`K_B = (4C + 2)/(4C − 3)`; solid length and total coils from Table 10-1;
buckling `y_cr = L₀ C₁′[1 − √(1 − C₂′/λ_eff²)]`, `λ_eff = α L₀/D`. Fatigue:
Zimmerli's `S_sa = 241`, `S_sm = 379 MPa` (unpeened; 398 / 534 peened) on a
Goodman line to `S_su = 0.67 S_ut`: `S_se = S_sa/(1 − S_sm/S_su)`,
`n_f = 1/(τ_a/S_se + τ_m/S_su)`. Finite life: the cycle is converted to the
zero-to-peak stress of equal Goodman damage, `S₀ = 2S_er/(1 + S_er/S_su)`, and
interpolated in the Table 10-8 cyclic allowables (36 / 33 / 30 % of S_ut at
10⁵ / 10⁶ / 10⁷ cycles unpeened, 42 / 39 / 36 % peened); below the 10⁷ value
the life is a runout. Extension: `N_a = N_b + G/E`, `L₀ = (2C − 1 + N_b)d`,
hook stresses with `(K)_A`, `(K)_B`, preferred initial stress
`231 e^{−0.105C} ± 6.9(4 − (C − 3)/6.5)` MPa. Torsion:
`k′ = d⁴E/(10.8 D N_a)` N·mm/turn with `N_a = N_b + (l₁ + l₂)/(3πD)`,
`σ = K_i 32M/(πd³)`, `K_i = (4C² − C − 1)/(4C(C − 1))`, `S_y ≈ 0.78 / 0.61 S_ut`.

**Gears.** The cutter is the ISO 53 basic rack (α, `h_fP = 1.25 m`, tip radius
`ρ_fP = 0.38 m`), its datum shifted `x m` from the rolling line. For radius ρ
the tooth space reaches angle `β(ρ) = max_u [u + (w(ρ cos u) − ρ sin u)/r]`
(w = the cutter's half-width at that height, flank or tip arc); the tooth
half-angle is `ψ = π/z − β`. On the flank this is exactly the involute
`ψ = π/(2z) + 2x tan α/z + inv α − inv α_ρ` (unit-tested to 2 × 10⁻⁷ rad);
below the form circle it is the generated trochoid, and undercut appears by
itself below `z_min = 2(1 − x)/sin²α`. Pairs: `inv α_w = inv α + 2 tan α (x₁ + x₂)/(z₁ + z₂)`,
`a_w = a cos α/cos α_w`, tip shortening `k = x₁ + x₂ − (a_w − a)/m`,
`ε_α = [√(r_a1² − r_b1²) + √(r_a2² − r_b2²) − a_w sin α_w]/(π m cos α)`.
Lewis: the parabola from the tip-load line's intersection with the tooth
centre line tangent to the fillet gives `x = t²/(4l)` and `Y = 2x/(3m)`;
`σ = K_v W_t/(b m Y)` with `K_v = (6.1 + V)/6.1` (Barth, cut teeth),
`(3.56 + √V)/3.56` (hobbed), `√((5.56 + √V)/5.56)` (shaved/ground),
`(3.05 + V)/3.05` (cast). AGMA 2001 / ISO 6336 add overload, dynamic, size,
load-distribution, rim-thickness and life factors, the geometry factor J (or
Y_F·Y_S with the fillet stress concentration) and a separate pitting check on
contact stress — use them for load-rated gears. Planetary (Willis):
`(ω_s − ω_c)/(ω_r − ω_c) = −Z_r/Z_s`; equal spacing needs `(Z_s + Z_r)/N`
integer; adjacency `(Z_s + Z_p) sin(π/N) > Z_p + 2`.

**Cams** (Norton ch. 8). Cycloidal `s = h[x − sin(2πx)/(2π)]`, peak
`a = 2πh/β²`; 3-4-5 `s = h(10x³ − 15x⁴ + 6x⁵)`, peak `5.7735 h/β²`; harmonic
`s = (h/2)(1 − cos πx)`, peak `π²h/(2β²)`. Translating roller follower:
`R_p = R_b + R_r`, `d = √(R_p² − ε²)`, pressure angle
`φ = atan((v − ε)/(d + s))`; pitch-curve curvature from its first and second
derivatives in follower coordinates, `P′ = (d + s, v − ε)`,
`P″ = (2v − ε, a − d − s)`; the cam surface is the pitch curve offset inward by
R_r (undercut when a convex pitch radius < R_r).

## Iris tie-in and metrics

Iris has a 5-barrel hinge with M1.4 screws per side (HW1, "spring hinge").
The lab models it as a compression-spring barrel ("flex") hinge — the
common eyewear spring hinge — using the CAD layout from the CAD Studio: the
stop offset `a = (KNUCKLE_LEN + KNUCKLE_GAP)/2 = 2.7 mm` behind the front,
the pin on the temple centre line (2 mm to either face of the 4 mm temple),
the ear contact at the ear bend 108.75 mm behind the pin and the pod ending
66.7 mm behind it. The temple end face has rounded corners (inner 1.1 mm,
outer 0.3 mm): it is the detent cam. The wire is 302 stainless (skin
contact), Ø0.45 mm, D = 2.0 mm (C = 4.4, OD 2.45 mm in a 4 mm temple), six
active coils, 5.5 mm free length, squared and ground, 0.6 mm preload
(k = 7.37 N/mm, 4.42 N). **Usage assumed:** 12 uses a day (open, wear,
close — two passes over the detent), 2 pulls to the 20° over-travel stop a
day, 3 years = 13 140 uses (the Structures Lab uses the same 12 open/close
per day for the plastic hinge area).

| Metric | Meaning | Iris value |
|---|---|---|
| `mechanisms.hinge_spring_sf` | Goodman (Zimmerli) fatigue safety factor, worst usage block | **1.40** (pulled to the stop: τ 331 → 587 MPa). Detent passes 1.92, worn on a 146 mm head 2.41. Shot-peened wire gives **1.90** |
| `mechanisms.hinge_life_cycles` | open/close uses to failure (10⁷ = runout cap) | **10⁷ (runout)** — every block is below the 10⁷-cycle allowable; need 13 140 |
| `mechanisms.hinge_open_torque_nmm` | torque to push the folded temple over the detent (with pin friction μ = 0.15) | **8.0 N·mm** (contact from 32°, hump at 56° with 0.28 mm slider travel); closing needs 4.4 N·mm |

Other numbers on the page: over-travel starts at 7.6 N·mm and stays almost
flat (the lever of the outer corner shrinks as the force grows); on a
146 mm head the hinge takes essentially all of the 12 mm spread per side
(α ≈ 6.3°) and clamps with only ≈ 0.08 N per side, against ≈ 17 N if the
hinge were rigid — so the spring hinge buys comfort and robustness, while
grip must come from temple bend, ear bend and nose pads.

**What the simulation caught:** with the first-choice unpeened wire, the
worst block (pulling the temples to the stop) gives n_f = 1.40 — below the
1.5 that Shigley's spring examples design to, although still infinite-life
on Zimmerli's line. Shot-peened wire (n_f 1.90), a 15° over-travel stop
(1.62) or 0.4 mm preload (1.57, but the detent falls to 5.4 N·mm) fixes it.

## Limitations

* Planar, rigid-body kinematics; no link inertia forces, joint clearances or
  elasticity. The four-bar is single-loop (no six-bars).
* Spring data are the published Shigley tables (Associated Spring and
  Zimmerli); fatigue scatters by a factor of 3–10 in life. Hard-drawn wire is
  not in Table 10-8 — the music-wire row is used (optimistic). Hook and
  torsion-spring fatigue use approximations stated on the page.
* Gears: spur only (no helical, internal, bevel or worm geometry beyond
  planetary pitch circles); Lewis + Barth is a sizing check, not a rating;
  allowables come from the Structures material table (fully reversed fatigue
  strength) — check supplier gear data, especially for printed or molded
  plastics. Tip-relief, crowning and transmission error are not modelled.
* Cams: translating roller follower only (no flat-faced or oscillating
  followers); no follower dynamics or spring sizing.
* Hinge: a frictionless virtual-work model of a flat stop face and round
  corners, plus pin friction; the real temple end, plating and wear change
  the feel. Temple stiffness is a two-segment beam (open-channel pod + arm);
  the Structures Lab finite element model is softer. The head width is a
  single number — use the Ergonomics (Human Factors) Lab for a population.
  ISO 12870 includes a hinge endurance test — check the current edition and
  run a cycling test on a prototype.

## Graduate to

Pyslvs-UI (`KmolYuan/Pyslvs-UI`, the closest open tool to the Linkages tab:
multi-loop linkages and evolutionary synthesis), David Rector's Linkage
program, MotionGen (Stony Brook), SolveSpace (`solvespace/solvespace`) for

(`BelfrySCAD/BOSL2`, `gears.scad` — studied for this lab: it builds the
involute from a lookup table and carves undercut with a sharp rack corner,

directly), the FreeCAD Gears workbench (`looooo/freecad.gears`), spring
makers' online calculators and catalogues, and AGMA 2001 / ISO 6336 rating
software for load-rated gears.
